# Script craft: an engineered, catchy script

A script that holds attention is built, not inspired. Use these in order; each rule has a reason and a test.

## 1. The hook (0-3 s): pattern interrupt + a promise

- **Contrast:** two short sentences that flip ("Stop editing videos. Start describing them."). The first names what they do now, the second the better way.
- **Direct address:** "you", imperative verbs. No "welcome to", no brand name yet.
- **Open a loop** the end will close: the prompt typed in the hook comes back rendered in the CTA.
- Test: read only the first 2 seconds aloud. Would a stranger scrolling stop?

## 2. The pain (3-7 s): rule of four, staccato

- Four one-word beats on the beat grid ("Timelines. Dragging. Exporting. Again."). Each word is its own hard cut and colour; the last word is the sting.
- Name the *verbs of the old way*, not feelings. Specific beats generic.
- Test: could a competitor's ad use the same words? Then they are too generic.

## 3. The turn: a visual metaphor, silent

- A short silent beat with a motion metaphor (two dots merge, split and grow into the next slide). The audio drops out, then lands the product on the drop.
- Test: remove the voiceover; can you still see the story turn?

## 4. The promise: name + one line

- "Meet <name>. <One concrete line>." The line is a fact about the product, not a benefit adjective ("One TSX file.").
- Match-cut the turn into it (a vault door opening on the circle the dots grew into).

## 5. The proof: specific, true, checkable

- Numbers only from the repo or the brief, and each one verified before it is on screen (39 tools from `tools/list`, 17 checks from `review`).
- Rule of three for features ("Licensed. Logged. Locked."); each word lands on its spoken beat.
- Never claim what you cannot show or measure. If a claim cannot be checked, cut it or rewrite it as something that can ("Diff it like code" instead of "same video every time").

## 6. The reveal and the CTA

- **Reframe:** one line that changes how they saw the film ("This whole ad is one TSX file.").
- **Callback:** close the loop opened in the hook.
- **One action, one command:** `npx flomotion`. Hold it for at least 1 s.

## Timing and voice

- 128 bpm: one beat = 0.46875 s, 64 beats = 30 s. Cuts on beats; words start on beats.
- 2.0-2.8 words per second. A line that needs more than 1.2x speed-up is too long: cut words, do not squeeze.
- One voice clip per phrase, placed on the beat, so the word times are exact.
- Say tricky words phonetically in the voice text ("flow-motion", "T S X") and map them back to the display spelling in the captions.
- Write copy that cannot be misheard: avoid heteronyms (e.g. "live" the adjective vs the verb); pick another word.

## The ten-second audit

1. Hook has a contrast and no brand name.
2. Pain is four specific words.
3. Turn works without sound.
4. Promise is a fact.
5. Every number is verified.
6. Three features max, in threes of words.
7. A reveal and a callback.
8. One CTA.
9. No word on screen that is not either spoken or a number.
10. No claim you could not show.
